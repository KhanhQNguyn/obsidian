## How I Approach Engineering

I tend to think about software at both the implementation level and the system level.

When working on a project, I naturally ask questions such as:

- What exactly are we building?
- What problem are we actually solving?
- Which features are essential, and which should be excluded?
- How should the system be structured?
- How should frontend and backend communicate?
- Who is best suited to each responsibility?
- How will individual components integrate?
- What could fail during the final demo or deployment?
- How can we make the final product reliable rather than merely complete?

Because of this, I often take on facilitator, integrator, or team-lead responsibilities while still contributing technically.

I am particularly interested in roles where I can remain close to engineering while also contributing to architecture, product decisions, and coordination.

## Learning Style

I learn most effectively through concrete problems and real projects.

My typical learning process is:

> **Encounter a problem → understand enough theory → build something → get stuck → investigate → fix the issue → understand the underlying concept more deeply**

I am comfortable entering projects without already knowing every required technology.

If a project requires an unfamiliar API, framework, protocol, development tool, AI service, or architecture, my instinct is usually to learn enough to make progress, apply it immediately, and then deepen my understanding through implementation.

This is one reason hackathons and project-based learning suit me well: they create an immediate reason to learn, experiment, and deliver.

I also strongly prefer practical explanations. I value theory, but I understand concepts best when I can connect them to a real engineering decision or implementation problem.
